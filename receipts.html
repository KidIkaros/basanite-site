1 · The independent audit: the spec fails its own goals

Golaszewski, Krawetz, Sherman et al. — arXiv:2604.24890, April 2026 · [H]

The first independent security analysis of C2PA concluded the specification "fails to achieve their claimed security goals." Load-bearing findings:

  • Revocation checking is optional for validators — and the spec permits OCSP only, expressly forbidding CRLs.
  • Timestamps are not bound into the signed claim the way the authors recommend; the paper's own fix proposal is exactly the binding our format does (checkpoints sign their own timestamps).
  • Exclusion ranges allow parts of a manifest to be marked unverifiable-and-ignored — a tamper surface.
  • Certificate expiry destroys verifiability of historical content (see incident 3).

Source: arxiv.org/abs/2604.24890 — fetched and read in full by this effort [H]; detailed companion study at IACR ePrint 2026/804.

2 · Revocation ignored in the field

Nikon Z6 III · revoked Nov 2025 · accepted ~6 months later · [H audit / M reporting]

A camera certificate that was revoked in November 2025 was still accepted by a major validator roughly six months later. Validators consulted gave contradictory results — one flagged the manifest invalid, the other showed it as good — and neither reported the revocation to the user (documented as Fig. 2 in the audit above).

Context: a separate signature-spoofing vulnerability in the same camera line days after its August 2025 launch led to certificates being revoked at all — the revocation machinery mattered, and the validators didn't run it. [M]

Sources: arXiv:2604.24890 [H] · Wikipedia, "Content Credentials" [M].

3 · Expiry destroyed verifiability of government records

Arizona Secretary of State pilot · validated Jan 2025, failed Jan 2026 · [M]

Content from an Arizona Secretary of State pilot validated in January 2025 and failed validation one year later — with the file unchanged. The cause was certificate expiry, not tampering: the verification model tied truth to a validity window rather than to the signed bytes. This is directly incompatible with records-retention obligations (the reporting cites 52 U.S.C. §20701 among others).

basanite's failure mode is the opposite shape: verification is offline and windowless — a manifest from years ago verifies today, and its footer discloses exactly what was and wasn't checked.

Source: Wikipedia, "Content Credentials" [M], citing the audit's expiry analysis.

4 · Stripping is the normal case, by the standard's own FAQ

C2PA FAQ / Editors Weblog adoption tracker · [M quotes / H design limitation]

Embedded manifests separate from content under ordinary use. The standard's own documentation and adoption tracking state, in summary:

  • "Email clients do not preserve C2PA. Messaging apps strip metadata. Most CMS platforms lack integration. The screenshot problem remains unsolved."
  • The BBC Verify pilot reported a video with fabricated audio that was silently republished three times with its manifest stripped — no alarm, no signal, because stripped means invisible, not invalid. [M]
  • The spec's fallback is soft bindings — content fingerprints and watermarking that require an online manifest-repository lookup to be useful, i.e. the exact platform dependency the standard's offline story disclaims.

Sources: c2pa.org/faqs · Editors Weblog adoption tracker [M] · spec §9 (bindings) [H].

  • Android signing forgery (Aug 2026): a demonstrated C2PA signing forgery from Android root access; Google declined to fix ("Won't fix — infeasible"). [M] — Wikipedia

What we do about it

Revocation: checked when a journal is present, disclosed when not

Every basanite verdict prints its revocation status — enforced against a healthy journal, or an explicit statement that it was not checked. "Absence of a journal is not evidence of absence of revocation" is printed verbatim in the footer rather than silently skipped. basanite verify file and read the footer.

Timestamps: inside the signature

Each checkpoint signs its own timestamp with strictly-increasing enforcement — the binding the independent audit recommends. Disclosed limitation: timestamps are signer-asserted, not TSA-attested; the footer says so on every verdict.

Verification: offline and windowless

No OCSP, no TSA, no manifest repository. A sealed file verifies today or in ten years from the same bytes. The honest counterpart, printed on every unanchored verdict: without the publisher's signed head anchor, a shortened history cannot be detected from the manifest alone.

Stripping: the history travels as a file, not a hope

The manifest is a plain sidecar file you distribute beside the asset — and the flagged-asset runbook covers recovery when a publisher omitted it. A missing manifest is reported as no-manifest, never as a pass.
