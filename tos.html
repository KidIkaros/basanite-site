2 · What basanite is — and is not

basanite records and verifies provenance histories: it seals the bytes of a file into a signed manifest, records subsequent edits, and lets anyone verify that the file's present bytes match the recorded history. That is all it does, and the product is explicit about everything it does not do.

  • It is not detection software. It does not tell you whether a file is "real", authentic, or truthful — it tells you whether the bytes match the recorded, signed history that accompanies them.
  • It is not a certification, notarization, or record of facts. Sealing a file proves when a key signed those bytes, not that the file's contents are accurate.
  • It operates offline and does not transmit your files, keys, or manifests anywhere. No telemetry.

3 · The three verdicts

Every verification ends in exactly one of three states, described in plain language on the docs page: manifest-intact (exit 0 — the bytes match the recorded history), manifest-invalid (exit 1 — they do not, or the history itself fails a check), and no-manifest (exit 1 — no claim exists). Each verdict prints what was checked and what was not, including revocation-journal status.

4 · What a verdict means (the clause that matters)

A verdict is a cryptographic statement about the manifest and the file it accompanies — not a guarantee of ground truth. "manifest-intact" means the file's present bytes match the signed history that was presented with it. It does not mean the file is authentic, unaltered before its first recorded signature, or that the events it depicts actually occurred. A file whose history was sealed from the start can still be a lie from before its first signature — an intact verdict on fabricated media proves only that the fabrication was recorded consistently.

  • Verdicts are only as meaningful as the recorded history presented with the file. Without the publisher's signed head anchor, a shortened history cannot be distinguished from a short one; every unanchored verdict says so in its output.
  • Verification is pinned to keys, not people. A roster or an embedded key binds a fingerprint to a verification; binding that fingerprint to a person or organization is an out-of-band act you perform, not one the software performs.
  • Verification with the publisher's key on your roster, an applied anchor, and a healthy revocation journal is the strongest verification the product can express — and it is still subject to this section.

5 · Your keys, your files, your responsibility

  • Seed files are the whole of your authority. A manifest is signed by whoever holds its seed; losing a seed means losing the ability to extend that history, and a stolen seed means someone else can (they still cannot rewrite existing history). Keep seeds offline and backed up. We cannot recover them — there is no server, so there is no reset.
  • Sidecar manifests are part of the work. Distribute the .opm (and any .anchor) alongside the asset, or a verifier has nothing to verify against. "no-manifest" is not "authentic".
  • You choose what to rely on. Verdicts are inputs to human judgment, not substitutes for it.
