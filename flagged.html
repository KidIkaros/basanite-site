Runbook · flagged assets

"Your product flagged
our asset." Good. Now prove it back.

A verification came back manifest-invalid on a file you received, published, or hold custody of. This page is the procedure: preserve evidence, re-verify with everything the publisher distributed, classify the failure, and write the custody note — before anyone edits, re-saves, or re-encodes anything.

The procedure

STEP 0

Freeze the evidence

Do not edit, re-save, re-encode, convert, or open-and-save the file. Any of those changes the bytes, and the bytes are the evidence. Work on copies from here on; keep the originals read-only.

STEP 1

Capture the full verdict, not the headline

Re-run verify and save the complete output, including the checked/not-checked footer:

# capture everything — verdict, reason, footer
basanite verify evidence.wav 2>&1; | tee verify-log.txt
sha256sum evidence.wav evidence.wav.opm evidence.wav.anchor 2>/dev/null | tee custody-hashes.txt

The reason: line is the diagnosis. The footer records what the tool could and could not check at that moment — that distinction matters later.

STEP 2

Re-verify with everything the publisher published

The default run checks only what sits beside the file. If the publisher distributed a signed head anchor or a revocation journal, re-run with them:

# full-strength verification
basanite verify evidence.wav \
  --sidecar evidence.wav.opm \
  --anchor  evidence.wav.anchor \
  --journal journal.jsonl

With the publisher's anchor applied, a trimmed or substituted history fails as manifest-invalid (head-anchor) instead of passing silently. With a journal present, keys the publisher revoked fail as revoked. Get these files from whoever published the asset — they are small, plain JSON, and meant to travel.

STEP 3

Classify the failure

Match the reason to the meaning (full definitions on what a verdict means):

manifest-invalid (content-hash: chunk N)
    → the file's present bytes differ from the sealed history.
    The change happened after sealing — by an edit that was never
    recorded, by corruption in transit, or by tampering.

manifest-invalid (head-anchor)
    → the history present is shorter than or different from what
    the publisher signed. Someone cut or rewrote the edit trail.

manifest-invalid (signature …)
    → a checkpoint signature does not verify. The manifest was
    forged or altered, or the signer's keys changed without rotation.

manifest-invalid (signer-revoked …)
    → a signing key on this history appears in the publisher's
    revocation journal.

no-manifest
    → no claim exists (or the sidecar was lost). Unverified is
    not authentic — but it is also not proof of fraud.
STEP 4

Write the chain-of-custody note

Before the file moves to anyone else — counsel, the platform, the other party — write down what you hold and how it reached you. Template:

CUSTODY NOTE — flagged asset
Date/time (UTC):        2026-__-__ __:__
Custodian:              [name, role, org]
Source of file:         [who sent it, channel, when; keep the original message]
Received checksum:      sha256: [from custody-hashes.txt]
Verified with:          basanite [version from `basanite --version`]
Verdict:                [headline line from verify-log.txt]
Reason:                 [reason line]
Footer limitations:     [what the footer said was NOT checked]
Anchor/journal used:    [yes/no — paths + their sha256]
Actions taken:          [none / copies made for: …]
Originals location:     [read-only storage path, who has access]
Contact on record:      [publisher/counterparty, channel]

Note: verdicts are cryptographic statements about the file's recorded
history, not findings of fact about who did what. This note records
evidence handling only.

That last line is the honesty clause, and it protects you: the verdict says the bytes differ from the sealed history. It does not say who changed them, when, or why — that determination belongs to the humans holding the custody chain.

STEP 5

Take the finding to the counterparty

If the asset came from someone else, the finding is theirs to answer: send them the verify log, the custody note, and a request for the original .opm / .anchor pair plus their seed-handling practices. A legitimate publisher can re-verify on their side in seconds and either produce matching evidence or acknowledge the discrepancy. If you are the publisher: re-run the same commands on your master copy, and if your copy verifies while the distributed copy does not, the divergence happened in distribution — the custody notes on both sides are how you find where.

Escalation

Newsroom tier: flagged-asset escalations go to support@ikaros.digital with the custody note and verify log attached — response-time promise per your agreement. Everyone else: the docs on this site plus the runbook above are the support channel for now; file handling at this tier is deliberately your hands, your tools, your records.

What basanite will not do

It will not tell you who changed a file, restore the original bytes, attribute intent, or certify that a sealed file is "true." It proves what happened to a file relative to its recorded history — and it refuses to guess about anything else. That refusal is the product. Everything above the line of cryptography is your record-keeping, and the custody note is what makes the verdict usable in front of a third party.
